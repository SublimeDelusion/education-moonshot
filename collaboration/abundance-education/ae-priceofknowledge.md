Why this matters in 2026

The cost of American higher education has become the defining economic question for
an entire generation — and AI is about to make it irrelevant.

In 1963, a year at a public university cost $243 — about two weeks of a median
household's income. Today it costs $10,753, more than two months. The inflation-
adjusted price has quadrupled. Student loan debt has reached $1.77 trillion, held

by 45 million Americans who will spend 20 years paying it off.

Meanwhile, every other essential service that technology has touched has gotten
cheaper. Genome sequencing fell from $100 million to $100. Solar energy fell from
$400 to $20 per megawatt-hour. AI inference costs collapsed from $60 to $0.05
per million tokens in five years. Education went the other direction — not because
it is inherently expensive, but because it is the one sector that has resisted the

technology transformation.

In 2026, the question is no longer whether the system is broken. It is whether AI
makes the system obsolete. When a motivated 18-year-old with an AI tutor and a
coding agent can become economically capable in three months instead of four
years, the $280,000 price tag on a traditional degree is not just expensive — it is a

competitive disadvantage.
