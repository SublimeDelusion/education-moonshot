## Why this matters in 2026

The cost of American higher education has become the defining economic question for an entire generation — and AI is about to make it irrelevant.

In 1963, a year at a public university cost $243 — about two weeks of a median household's income. Today it costs $10,753, more than two months. The inflation-adjusted price has quadrupled. Student loan debt has reached $1.77 trillion, held by 45 million Americans who will spend 20 years paying it off.

Meanwhile, every other essential service that technology has touched has gotten cheaper. Genome sequencing fell from $100 million to $100. Solar energy fell from $400 to $20 per megawatt-hour. AI inference costs collapsed from $60 to $0.05 per million tokens in five years. Education went the other direction — not because it is inherently expensive, but because it is the one sector that has resisted the technology transformation.

In 2026, the question is no longer whether the system is broken. It is whether AI makes the system obsolete. When a motivated 18-year-old with an AI tutor and a coding agent can become economically capable in three months instead of four years, the $280,000 price tag on a traditional degree is not just expensive — it is a competitive disadvantage.

The data on this page tells the full story: where the money goes, who stopped paying, why costs rose, and what exponential technology means for all of it. Every chart is sourced. Every number can be checked.

## Where your tuition actually goes

Less than a quarter of what you pay goes directly to teaching you. The rest is administration, research, buildings, and things that have nothing to do with your classroom experience.

### Average public university spending breakdown, FY 2021–22

| Category | Share | Page description |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Instruction | 24.67% | Faculty salaries, TAs, departmental costs. This is what you think you are paying for. Less than a quarter of the budget. |
| Research | 14.58% | Sponsored research, labs, indirect cost recovery. Mostly funded by federal grants, but tuition subsidizes the gap. |
| Public Service & Admin | 11.78% | Central administration, executive compensation, legal, HR, marketing. The fastest-growing category since 1990. |
| Student Services | 7.84% | Counseling, career services, student activities, health centers. Growing fast as universities add non-academic services. |
| Scholarships & Grants | 8.24% | Institutional aid — tuition discounting. Universities raise sticker price then discount it. The net price is what matters. |
| Auxiliary & Other | 8.15% | Housing, dining, athletics, bookstores. Most athletic departments lose money. Students subsidize through mandatory fees. |

Source stated on page: NCES IPEDS Table 334.10, Delta Cost Project; public four-year institutions, all funds.

## The human cost of student debt

Student debt does not just affect bank accounts. It delays marriages, prevents homeownership, suppresses entrepreneurship, and harms mental health. The effects compound across decades.

### How student debt delays life milestones

| Reported impact | Share encoded in chart |
| --- | ---: |
| Delay homeownership | 83% |
| Delay marriage | 33% |
| Delay children | 29% |
| Delay retirement savings | 58% |
| Reduce entrepreneurship | 25% |
| Worsen mental health | 47% |

Sources stated on page: National Association of Realtors, Pew Research, Federal Reserve Survey of Consumer Finances. Percentage of borrowers reporting each impact.

The four accompanying cards state:

- **36% — Delay homeownership.** “Student debt holders are 83% of non-homeowners cite student debt as delaying purchase. . The National Association of Realtors cites student debt as the #1 reason millennials do not buy homes.”
- **-25% — Suppress entrepreneurship.** People with student debt are 25% less likely to start a business. A $1,000 increase in debt reduces the probability of starting a business by 1–2%. The Kauffman Foundation calls this the “debt overhang” effect.
- **4 yrs — Delay marriage.** Student debt delays marriage by an average of 3–4 years. 28–33% of borrowers delay marriage or children. Birth rates among 25–34 year olds correlate with student debt levels at the state level.
- **2x — Mental health toll.** Borrowers are twice as likely to experience anxiety and depression. Borrowers are 3x more likely to report suicidal ideation. Financial stress is the #1 reported concern among student borrowers.
