## Introduction

How federal policy, financial crises, and exponential technology reshaped American higher education — from 1965 to 2025.

## 🏛 The Foundation Era

Federal investment builds the modern higher education system

### 1965

**Higher Education Act** creates federal student loans and grants, opening college doors to millions of Americans for the first time. Lyndon Johnson signs it as part of the Great Society.

Tag: Policy

### 1972

**Pell Grants created** — the cornerstone of federal student aid. Named after Senator Claiborne Pell, these grants provide need-based funding that doesn't need to be repaid.

Tag: Policy

### 1978

**Middle Income Student Assistance Act** expands federal loan access beyond low-income families to middle-class students, dramatically increasing the eligible pool of borrowers.

Tag: Policy

## 🔥 The Cost Spiral Begins

State funding retreats while the Bennett Hypothesis predicts what's coming

### 1980

**Recession hits state budgets** — the first wave of state funding cuts to public universities. The pattern that will define the next four decades begins: states cut, universities raise tuition.

Tag: Crisis

### 1987

**William Bennett publishes the Bennett Hypothesis:** increases in financial aid enable colleges to raise tuition. "Federal student aid policies have made it easier for colleges to raise their tuitions." The debate rages to this day.

Tag: Crisis

## ⛓ The Debt Machine

Loan expansion + bankruptcy protection = an industry with no price discipline

### 1992

**Student loan limits raised, unsubsidized loans created.** Students can now borrow regardless of need — a blank check that removes any remaining price ceiling on tuition.

Tag: Crisis

### 1998

**Bankruptcy reform** makes student loans nearly impossible to discharge. Unlike credit card debt, mortgages, or business loans, student debt follows you forever.

Tag: Crisis

### 2005

**Bankruptcy Abuse Prevention Act** makes ALL student loans — including private — completely non-dischargeable. Lenders now face zero risk. The lending spigot opens wide.

Tag: Crisis

## ⚡ Technology Deflation Begins

While education costs rise, technology costs start their exponential decline

### 2003

**Human Genome Project completed** at a cost of ~$2.7 billion. Genome sequencing will follow a deflation curve: $100M → $1M → $1,000 → $100 by 2025 — faster than Moore's Law.

Tag: Technology

### 2008

**Financial crisis** devastates state budgets. Public universities face their deepest funding cuts ever. Students absorb the difference: average tuition jumps 15% in two years.

Tag: Crisis

### 2010

**Health Care and Education Reconciliation Act** switches federal lending to direct lending, eliminating bank middlemen. Saves ~$60B over a decade. **Solar crosses $1/watt** — a technology cost milestone that will accelerate the deflation narrative.

Tag: Inflection

### 2012

**Student debt crosses $1 trillion** for the first time — surpassing credit card debt. The average graduate carries $29,400 in loans. The crisis enters mainstream consciousness.

Tag: Crisis

## 🤖 The AI Inflection

Artificial intelligence begins its exponential cost decline

### 2016

**AlphaGo defeats Lee Sedol**, signaling AI's arrival as a transformative technology. AI inference costs begin an exponential decline that will eventually make knowledge work nearly free.

Tag: Technology

### 2020

**COVID-19 accelerates online learning** by a decade overnight. Every university goes remote. The myth that education requires physical presence cracks. Students question what they're actually paying for.

Tag: Inflection

### 2023

**Supreme Court strikes down Biden loan forgiveness.** The political system fails to address the $1.77T crisis. Meanwhile, **GPT-4 costs $30/M tokens** — expensive, but the exponential decline has begun.

Tag: Inflection

## 🔮 The Convergence

Technology deflation meets education cost inflation

### 2025

**The convergence arrives.** AI threatens traditional degrees. Genome sequencing costs $100 (from $2.7B). Token costs fall to $0.05/M (from $30/M in 2023). Average education costs $10,753/year — still climbing. The question is no longer *if* technology disrupts education, but *how fast*.

Tag: Future
