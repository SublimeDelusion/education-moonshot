# AI Is Part of the Education

**Students should learn to work extensively and effectively with AI because directing that capability is part of the professional education they need. AI use belongs inside the curriculum, the work, and the assessment.**

## Make AI a capability students develop

The model's response to AI is to require students to learn how to use it. Students need repeated experience framing tasks, selecting tools, supplying context, delegating work, inspecting evidence, and challenging results.

That experience should begin in Year One and become more sophisticated as the student's profession and responsibilities develop. Occasional permission to use a chatbot on an old assignment would leave too much of this education to chance.

Students will need to know when an AI system has answered the question, when it has missed the question, and when the question itself is poorly chosen. They need to distinguish persuasive language from evidence and learn to make a decision for which they will remain responsible.

## Two connected uses

AI participates in both learning and production.

As a **learning system**, it tutors, generates practice, exposes misconceptions, offers alternative explanations, and rehearses difficult questions.

As a **working system**, it helps research, analyze, model, organize, design, and produce. The student learns to coordinate this work and judge its relevance and quality.

These roles develop together. A student can use AI to build a model and then use it as a tutor to understand the model's behavior. The university's assessment determines whether that understanding actually belongs to the student.

The amount of machine assistance is therefore a poor substitute for evidence of learning. We need to examine what the student understands, directs, checks, and can take responsibility for.

## The student owns the judgment

Delegation is itself a professional skill. A capable student knows what to ask for, what context is missing, which assumptions to examine, and what deserves an expert's attention.

The human remains responsible for the reasoning and result. That includes recognizing the limits of their own understanding. A student cannot resolve an objection merely by reporting that the AI supplied the answer.

The narrative makes this visible twice. During Anya's early defense, AI provides an answer she cannot explain; she must return to learning. During Nora's professional evaluation, she rejects an AI recommendation because it misses a consequential contract term. These scenes illustrate different stages of the same responsibility.

## Keep the connected questions separate

There are three further decisions that follow from making AI part of education:

1. **What should students now be expected to accomplish?** That is the question of [higher learning standards](https://github.com/SublimeDelusion/education-moonshot/blob/5a20709ac09680b032322a2c45e2ddb8d6938151/concepts/higher-capability-higher-standards.md).
2. **How do we know they understand?** That is the question of [assessment](https://github.com/SublimeDelusion/education-moonshot/blob/5a20709ac09680b032322a2c45e2ddb8d6938151/concepts/assessment-through-application-and-defense.md).
3. **How does someone outside the institution judge them?** That is the question of [professional proof](https://github.com/SublimeDelusion/education-moonshot/blob/5a20709ac09680b032322a2c45e2ddb8d6938151/concepts/the-work-is-the-proof.md).

Banning AI would avoid developing the capability at the center of all three. Keeping AI available does not answer those questions by itself. The institution must redesign the work and inspect the student's understanding.

## Teaching the practice

The current model includes a dedicated first-year class in AI use and the formation of a persistent agent force. Students learn with general-purpose models and specialist systems appropriate to their subjects. They preserve sources, corrections, experiments, and reasoning that future work can use.

That preparation then becomes normal professional practice. Students bring AI into defenses, founder reviews, capstone work, and employer evaluations. Faculty and external reviewers can watch how the student directs and challenges it.

The governing educational stance is extensive, deliberate use with visible human responsibility.

## Questions to develop

The exact technical systems and instructional methods remain open. The model still needs to specify how faculty recognize dependence that is preventing learning, how the tutor challenges rather than flatters the learner, and how students learn the limits of different tools. These questions should improve the education students receive with AI.
