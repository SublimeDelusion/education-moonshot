# Learning design

**Status:** Exploratory. These concepts preserve the imported working notes; inclusion does not adopt a mechanism or settle an open question.

Current proposed design: [operating model](../../src/model/operating-model.md) and [founding principles](../../src/model/founding-principles.md).

---

## Purpose turns learning from push into pull

Traditional education often pushes a predetermined body of knowledge toward students in anticipation of future need.

The student naturally asks:

> When am I ever going to use this?

University of Impact reverses the causal chain.

The student begins with something they genuinely want to accomplish.

The work creates a need.

The need pulls knowledge toward the student.

The learning loop becomes:

**purpose → problem → knowledge needed → learning → application → feedback → deeper problem**

The student learns because the knowledge has become necessary.

Purpose is therefore not a motivational wrapper around a conventional curriculum.

It changes the direction of learning.

---

## Immediate application is part of the learning mechanism

Self-directed learning is not merely personalized pacing.

The student learns a concept because the work requires it and then applies it immediately.

Reality becomes part of the educational feedback loop.

A weak mental model is exposed by a failed experiment, a rejected design, a broken financial assumption, an unhappy user, or an expert who challenges the reasoning.

The delay between learning and application should be compressed whenever possible.

---

## Year Two creates a living library of applications

Year Two retains serious professional depth and a shared disciplinary corpus.

The difference is that every student brings a different mission and therefore a different application of the same professional knowledge.

Thirty students may learn the same engineering principle, financial model, biological mechanism, or design method and immediately apply it across dozens of different industries and problems.

The class therefore sees more than the one canonical example selected for a textbook.

It becomes a living library of applications.

This may give students something many working professionals do not acquire early in their careers:

**deep expertise in one profession combined with broad cross-industry pattern recognition.**

Students learn which principles generalize, which assumptions are contextual, and how the same professional idea behaves under different constraints.

Personalization should therefore make the cohort richer, not isolate students from one another.
